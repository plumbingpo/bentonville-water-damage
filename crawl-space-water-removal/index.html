Crawl Space Water Removal in Bentonville

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.
