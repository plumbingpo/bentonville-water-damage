Typical Water Damage Problems in Jonesboro

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Here is what homeowners around Jonesboro phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Jonesboro properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. In Jonesboro we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

How Long Drying Takes in a Jonesboro Home

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Jonesboro property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. For most Jonesboro homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

The First Hour After Water Damage in Jonesboro

The right first steps change with the type of water on your floor. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. It is fine to step through in footwear, towel it up and carry your things clear. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. Sewage and floodwater from outdoors are a health hazard, and the only job for you is to close the door and keep everyone out.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Jonesboro crew start faster. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Write down when you found the water and what you think caused it. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. After it has sat in building materials it degrades, and mold can take hold in 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Jonesboro leak seems small.
