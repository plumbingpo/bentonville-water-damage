A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Bentonville, Bentonville Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
