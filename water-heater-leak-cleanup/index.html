Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Bentonville

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Bentonville, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.
